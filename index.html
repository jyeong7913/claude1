Safety

Travel Smart

Essential safety notes for every destination in our guide.

General Travel Safety
  • Always purchase comprehensive travel insurance before departure
  • Keep digital and physical copies of your passport, visa, and tickets
  • Register with your embassy if staying more than 30 days
  • Share your itinerary with a trusted contact at home
  • Use hotel safes for passports and valuables
  • Carry a small emergency cash reserve in local currency
Southeast Asia
  • Drink only bottled water in Thailand, Vietnam, Indonesia, and Philippines
  • Negotiate taxi fares before boarding or insist on the meter
  • Be alert to gem scams and too-good-to-be-true tour offers
  • Check visa requirements — many countries require advance e-visa
  • Vaccinations: Hepatitis A, Typhoid, and Malaria prevention for rural areas
  • Emergency: 112 (international) works in most SE Asian countries
East Asia (Japan, Korea, Taiwan)
  • Among the world's safest destinations for tourists
  • Japan: earthquakes — know your hotel's emergency procedure
  • Download a translation app — English signage is limited outside cities
  • Tap water is safe to drink in Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan
  • Japan emergency: Police 110 / Ambulance & Fire 119
  • Korea emergency: Police 112 / Ambulance 119
Oceania (Sydney / Australia)
  • Sun protection is critical — UV index regularly reaches extreme levels
  • Swim only at patrolled beaches between the flags
  • Check for fire danger ratings if visiting national parks
  • Australia emergency: 000 (Police, Ambulance, Fire)
  • Travel insurance is strongly recommended for medical cover
  • Hire car? Drive on the left — different for most Asian travellers
Maldives & Island Resorts
  • Respect local customs — conservative dress outside resort islands
  • Alcohol is only permitted on resort islands, not local islands
  • Check seaplane / speedboat transfer schedules carefully
  • Marine safety: do not touch coral — heavy fines apply
  • Emergency: 119 (Maldives National Helpline)
  • Travel insurance with medical evacuation is essential
Health & Medications
  • Carry a doctor's letter for prescription medications
  • Many medications require prescriptions in Asian countries
  • Bring enough supply for your entire trip plus extra
  • Consider altitude sickness meds if trekking at elevation
  • Mosquito repellent (DEET 30%+) for tropical destinations
  • Consult a travel doctor 6–8 weeks before departure
